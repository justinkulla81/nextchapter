## Post 4: Colleges and AI, by career stage (company page; best with a screenshot of the colleges table)

> Every college now has an AI program. Almost none can tell you what it's worth.
>
> We mapped how U.S. colleges are responding to AI at every career stage:
>
> 🎓 Undergrad: Colleges awarding AI bachelor's degrees went from 4 to 23 in five years. Purdue now requires AI competency of every new undergraduate.
> 💻 Mid-career: Online AI master's from UT Austin (~$10K), Georgia Tech (under $7K) and CU Boulder ($15,750, no application).
> 📊 MBA: Harvard Business School made AI a required course. Wharton has an AI major.
> 🏛️ Executive ed: $3,850 online at MIT, up to $18,500 for six days at Stanford.
> ⚖️ Professional degrees: law schools requiring AI certification; medical schools teaching AI went from 53% to 77% in a year.
>
> What's missing: we found no rigorous data on jobs or pay for graduates of the new AI degrees. The best evidence (an Oxford hiring experiment) shows AI skills raise interview invitations by 8–15 points, not pay.
>
> Our read for experienced professionals: start with the cheapest options (free alumni courses, low-cost online degrees). The priciest have the least evidence.
>

## Post 5: The career-ladder slowdown (personal post from Justin)

> The career ladder is getting shorter, and it's not only about layoffs.
>
> Payroll and HR data from five different companies point the same way:
>
> • Promotions fell in 10 of 11 industries (Workday).
> • Small-business promotion rates fell from 14.6% to 10.3% (Gusto).
> • Managers now oversee about 6 people, up from 3–3.5 in 2019 (Gusto).
> • Middle-management job postings are down 42% from their 2022 peak (Revelio Labs).
> • 1 in 4 mid-career professionals has gone 5+ years without a promotion or meaningful raise (Burning Glass Institute and NYU).
>
> Fewer promotions inside means fewer openings below, which matters for the people already looking.
>
> Is your company promoting less, or just promoting differently?

## Post 6: The retraining gap (company page)

> If you lose a six-figure job, what does public retraining offer you?
>
> The numbers from the federal Dislocated Worker program (latest year):
>
> 👥 About 187,000 people served nationwide
> 📚 Only about 1 in 5 got training
> 💵 About $7,300 spent per trainee
> 💼 Median earnings afterward: about $40,000 a year
>
> Funding is flat. Congress is debating deep cuts. The new AI retraining grants mostly target manufacturing, energy and construction. Short-term Pell grants approved so far cover EMTs and medical assistants.
>
> For comparison, about 546,000 managers and professionals have been out of work for 6+ months this year.
>
> The public system was built for middle-wage reemployment. For laid-off professionals, the gap gets filled by severance, outplacement, alumni programs and their own savings, or not at all.
>
