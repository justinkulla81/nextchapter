# NextChapter — Intake Triage & Networking Design

Version 0.1 — which profile fields go upfront, which wait, and how to handle networking head-on.

---

## 1. The four goals, and what each actually needs

| Goal | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **A. Accurate difficulty weighting** | Target, location + mobility, search duration, application/interview funnel, comp floor relative to level |
| **B. Prospecting help** | Networking comfort, the real hesitation behind it, public visibility willingness |
| **C. Less blame on the AI** | Constraints visible in the explanation — location, comp, life logistics, direction |
| **D. Buy-in without overwhelm** | Few questions, fast, and about *them* rather than about scoring |

A and C pull toward more questions. D pulls toward fewer. The resolution is tiering — and recognizing that **not everything needs to gate the score.** Only the inputs the score is actually wrong without.

---

## 2. Tier 1 — before the first score

Five questions. Target 90 seconds. No typing except optional edits.

| # | Question | Format | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Target role, level, industry** — pre-filled from the resume, confirm or edit | Confirm | A, C |
| 2 | **Location and mobility** — location pre-filled; ask relocation / remote / radius | Confirm + 1 tap | A, C |
| 3 | **How long have you been searching?** | 4 options | A |
| 4 | **Applications sent, and any interviews yet?** | 2 taps | A |
| 5 | **How do you feel about networking?** | 4 options | B, D |

### Why these five

**1–2 are non-negotiable.** Target Fit, relevance decay, and market volume are literally uncomputable without them. Scoring anyway is what produced the Adrienne failure.

**3–4 are the diagnostic funnel.** Highest information per second of anything in the form. They locate the actual failure point, and they cost nothing for a brand-new searcher.

**5 is here for a specific reason.** Networking is the highest-leverage channel above director level and the one candidates most avoid. Asking on the way in — before any grade lands — makes it a conversation about them rather than a grade on their behavior. It also lets the first report speak to their actual resistance instead of scolding them.

### What's deliberately not in Tier 1

**Comp floor.** Genuinely valuable for difficulty and for blame-shifting, but it's the most sensitive question in the form and the likeliest drop-off point. Move it to Tier 2, immediately after the score, where the candidate has already received value.

**Seniority reached.** Inferable from the resume. Confirm in Tier 2 rather than ask.

**Anything emotional.** See §5.

## 3. Tier 2 — immediately after the first score

Framed as *sharpen this*, not *complete your profile*. The candidate has a result in hand; now they're improving its accuracy. Motivation is much higher here than before the score.

| Field | Why here |
|---|---|
| Compensation floor | Big difficulty driver; sensitive enough to need trust first |
| Seniority reached | Confirm the resume inference |
| Target industries | Pre-filled from past industries; confirm/edit |
| Function, and whether they're pivoting | Drives job matching |
| Company size preference | Narrows matching |
| **The real hesitation behind networking** | The unlock for §4 |
| Public visibility willingness | Gates the Marketing Plan and LinkedIn generator |
| Direction — same path, or something different | Materially changes targeting and difficulty |
| Willingness to follow up on applications | Cheap, high-yield behavior |
| Weekly outreach target | Sets the Sprint |

## 4. Tier 3 — progressive, at the moment of use

Never a wall. Each surfaces when it would change what happens next.

- Blockers (ATS, skills gap, bias, interview nerves, career gap, life logistics, local market, confidence)
- Behavioral patterns (procrastination, stalling, embarrassment, financial pressure, uncertainty about direction)
- Consistency self-report
- Motivations, and the free-text "what gets you up in the morning"
- Coaching tone and pace preference
- Priority ranking across comp, brand, stability, work-life, mission
- Benefits and non-salary preferences
- Posting frequency and enjoyment

**Cut or make clearly optional:** the 1–5 priority ranking is high-friction drag-and-drop for low immediate value — ask it when an offer is actually in play. The benefits yes/no battery belongs at offer stage, not intake.

## 5. Handling networking head-on

### 5.1 The two questions

**"How do you feel about reaching out to people?"** (Tier 1) — Very comfortable / Somewhat / Not very / I'd rather not.

**"What's the real hesitation?"** (Tier 2) — the six-option list already in the form. This is the best-designed question in the whole intake, because each answer maps to a *different* intervention. A single "networking is at F" grade can't do that.

### 5.2 Hesitation → intervention

| They say | What we say | What we give them |
|---|---|---|
| **I don't want to seem desperate** | You're not asking for a job. You're asking for a read on the market from someone who knows it. That's a normal professional conversation. | Script that asks for perspective, not a role |
| **I don't want to burden people** | People who like you generally want to help and rarely get asked. The ask is fifteen minutes, and most say yes. | Short, low-obligation opener with an easy out |
| **I'm not sure what to say** | The easiest one to fix — this is a wording problem, not a courage problem. | Ready-to-send script, named person, editable |
| **My network isn't strong enough** | It's almost always larger than it feels. Former colleagues, your board, alumni, vendors, people you hired. | Generated list from their own history, ranked by warmth |
| **I don't like asking for help** | Then don't. Offer something instead — most of these conversations run both directions. | Reciprocal-framing script |
| **I've already used up my network** | Re-contacting is normal and expected. The second conversation is a different ask: specific, not general. | Re-contact cadence plus a narrowed ask |
| **Something else** | Free text, routed to the coach | — |

### 5.3 Tone

Confront it directly, without moralizing:

> Most searches at your level are won or lost here. You said you'd rather not reach out — that's the most common answer we get, and it's worth naming what's actually behind it, because the fix is different depending on the reason.

Never grade the discomfort. Name it, then solve for it.

### 5.4 Deduplicate

"I'm shy, or I don't like networking" currently appears in the behavioral blockers list *and* in the networking section. Keep it once, in the networking section, where it has an intervention attached.

## 6. Cautions

**Keep completion points visually separate from the score.** The form shows "+5 pts," "+10 pts," "20 points left." If those read as improving the Market Reality Grade, the grade stops meaning difficulty and starts meaning form-filling — which breaks the honesty of the whole thing. Profile completeness is its own progress indicator, in its own visual language, never blended into the grade.

**Delay the emotional-state question.** The 1–4 scale ending in "Honestly, I'm getting desperate" is genuinely useful for coaching tone, and it's a hard thing to ask someone on the day they were fired. Tier 3, after trust exists.

**Route the high-distress answers.** "Getting desperate," "I need money soon," and "embarrassed being unemployed" are wellbeing signals. Handle them consistently with the existing Support During Transition boundary — surfaced as an offer the candidate chooses, never as automated detection or intervention.

**Pre-fill everything possible.** Past industries, location, function, and seniority are all inferable from the resume. The form already pre-fills industries — extend that pattern everywhere. Confirming a guess converts far better than filling a blank, and it proves the resume was read.

**Every question must change an output.** If an answer doesn't change the score, the plan, the matching, or the coaching voice, cut it. That test alone would shorten this form considerably.
