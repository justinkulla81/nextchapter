Covers: Confidential Search Mode, how references work under it, the prompt to move from passive to active searching, and the weekly leaderboards.

# PART ONE — CONFIDENTIAL SEARCH MODE

## 1. Why a mode, not a persona setting

A candidate who selected **"I'm worried I'll be let go" is still employed.** If their search becomes visible they can lose the job they still have, forfeit severance, or damage a relationship they'll need as a reference.

The need isn't limited to that persona. Someone who resigned may be on garden leave. Someone laid off may still want discretion. Someone worried today may be laid off next month.

**It is a mode anyone can toggle, defaulted ON for the Worried and Resigned personas.** Persona sets the default; the member controls it.

## 2. The hard rule

> **In Confidential Search Mode, nothing the member does is visible to anyone outside NextChapter, and nothing NextChapter sends identifies them as job-searching.**

Not a preference to be balanced against engagement. It overrides growth features, gamification, and defaults everywhere.

## 3. Defaults

| Persona | Default |
|---|---|
| I'm worried I'll be let go | **ON** |
| I just resigned | **ON** — garden leave, notice periods, reference risk |
| I was laid off | OFF |
| I'm re-entering the workforce | OFF |

Turning it **off** shows a confirmation naming exactly what becomes visible. Turning it **on** is immediate and needs no confirmation.

## 4. Surface by surface

### 4.1 Leaderboards
**Hard exclusion.** Not defaulted off — excluded, and the opt-in control is not shown. They still see their own rank, personal bests, and badges privately. Badges are earned normally and simply never display publicly.

### 4.2 Community
Generated handle only; name mode unavailable. No employer, title, or location on the profile. Warning on first post: *anything you write here is visible to other members.*

### 4.3 Marketing Plan and LinkedIn
- LinkedIn posting **disabled by default**, with the reason stated
- "Open to Work" badge guidance **suppressed entirely** — never recommended
- Profile optimization still offered, filtered to changes that don't signal a search: no "seeking new opportunities," no availability language
- If they enable posting deliberately, warn once: *your current employer can see this*

### 4.4 Outreach
- Scripts default to **discreet framing** — asking for market perspective, never announcing a search
- Never suggest mass or broadcast outreach
- Flag contacts who currently work at their employer before sending

### 4.5 Email and notifications
- **Neutral subject lines.** Nothing containing "job search," "resume," "application," "interview," or "career transition." Work email and shared screens are the threat model.
- Never send to a work address. If the registered address is a corporate domain, warn and request a personal one.
- Push notification previews suppressed.

### 4.6 Gmail connection — hard block on corporate domains

Connecting a **work** Google account would place the entire job search inside the employer's systems, where it is typically discoverable.

> **Use a personal account.** Connecting your work email would put your job search inside your employer's systems, where they can see it. We'll only connect a personal account while Confidential Search Mode is on.

### 4.7 Dossier and recruiter introductions
- Dossier **never shared without explicit per-instance consent.** A general permission is not enough.
- Every recruiter introduction names the firm and requires a yes for that specific introduction
- Current employer never appears in any outbound distribution

### 4.8 Applications
Warn before applying to their current employer or a known subsidiary.

## 5. What the mode does not change

- Full Market Reality Report and every component grade
- Resume Studio, all versions, all tailoring
- All assessments, **all references** (see Part Two), the full Dossier
- Job matching and applications
- Coaching
- Badges, points, streaks, personal bests — earned and displayed **to them**

**The mode removes visibility, never capability.** No member should feel they got a lesser product for needing discretion. No nudging toward turning it off, no "you're missing out on the leaderboard."

## 6. Interface

Persistent, quiet indicator wherever visibility could be at stake:

> 🔒 **Confidential Search Mode is on.** Nothing you do here is visible to other members or outside NextChapter. [Manage]

Settings screen lists plainly what is and isn't visible. No dark patterns.

# PART TWO — REFERENCES UNDER CONFIDENTIAL MODE

## 7. References are required for everyone

**Do not skip or soften the reference ask for passive or confidential members.** References are the single largest lever in the product and the core of the Dossier. A member who never collects them never gets a Dossier, regardless of why they're searching quietly.

A former colleague from three jobs ago is not a channel back to a current employer. The risk is manageable with sourcing and wording, not by skipping the step.

## 8. Sourcing rules

| Mode | Suggested sources |
|---|---|
| Confidential | **Former** colleagues, managers, board members, clients. Current-employer contacts require an explicit extra confirmation. |
| Open | Anyone, including current colleagues |

Flag any suggested contact who currently works at the member's employer, and require a second confirmation naming the risk before sending.

## 9. Request wording adapts to mode

The reference request email itself must not expose the search.

**Confidential mode:**

> Subject: A quick favor
>
> Marcus is putting together a professional profile and asked whether you'd be willing to share your perspective on working with him. It takes about ten minutes and covers how he operates, not whether he's looking for anything.

**Open mode:**

> Subject: Reference request from Marcus Hollander
>
> Marcus is in a job search and asked whether you'd serve as a reference.

Both are honest. The confidential version is accurate — a passive candidate *is* building a profile — and it doesn't announce a search to someone who might mention it.

**The reference never learns the member's current employer** beyond what the member chooses to include.

# PART THREE — PASSIVE TO ACTIVE

## 10. The prompt

Members in Confidential Search Mode are often genuinely passive at first and genuinely active later. The product should notice and ask — once, without pressure.

**Shown only to members in Confidential Search Mode.**

> **Has anything changed?**
>
> You've been searching quietly, which makes sense when you're still employed. If your situation has shifted, opening up unlocks a few things: posting to LinkedIn, the leaderboards, the community under your own name, and broader outreach.
>
> [Something's changed — open up my search] · [Not yet] · [Don't ask again]

## 11. Triggers

Show it when there's an actual reason, never on a timer alone:

| Trigger | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Dossier reaches complete | They're ready — this is the natural moment |
| First application logged | They're acting, not just watching |
| Weekly activity doubles versus their trailing average | Behavior changed |
| Persona changed from Worried to Laid off | Situation changed. **Prompt, never auto-switch.** |
| 60 days elapsed with no prompt shown | Low-frequency check-in |

**Rules:** never more than once every 30 days. "Don't ask again" is permanent and honored. Never gate anything on the answer. Never imply passive searching is inadequate — for a still-employed candidate, it is the correct strategy.

## 12. The reverse

Turning the mode back **on** is always available in one tap from settings, and required if their situation reverses — a new role, a changed circumstance. No confirmation, no friction, immediate effect.

# PART FOUR — WEEKLY LEADERBOARDS

## 13. Points are not the grade

**Action Points are an activity currency. They are not the Market Reality Grade.** Points measure what someone did this week; the grade measures how hard their search is and how strong a case we can make. Different visual language, different surfaces, never blended. If points appear to move the grade, the grade stops meaning anything.

## 14. The boards

Five reset weekly on Monday; one is lifetime.

| Board | Counts |
|---|---|
| **Action Score** | Total weekly Action Points |
| **Outreach** | Distinct-recipient messages sent |
| **Applications** | Applications logged |
| **Visibility** | LinkedIn posts published through the Marketing Plan |
| **Contribution** | Community posts that received a reply or reaction |
| **Lifetime Action Score** | Cumulative, all-time |

### 14.1 Applications: effort, not strategy

We cannot verify that an application used a tailored version or fell inside the stated target. We trust the member and count what they log.

| Surface | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Leaderboard** | Effort. Hard work deserves recognition regardless of how optimally it's allocated. |
| **Market Reality Grade** | Strategy. Whether the effort is working (master script §3.9). |

A member can top the applications board and carry a scattershot penalty in their grade. Both statements are true: they worked hard at something that isn't converting.

**The tension is addressed in the report and by the coach, never on the board.** The board congratulates. The report says: *"You led the board on applications this week and none of them converted — let's change where that effort goes."*

## 15. Segmentation and privacy

**Segment by seniority band**, matching Community. A President and an early-career candidate racing on outreach volume is meaningless in both directions. Optional secondary filter by function.

**Top 10 shown publicly.** Never a full ranking.

**Opt-in, default off.** Three display modes: first name + last initial · generated handle · not listed. **Never displayed:** employer, title, location, industry.

Members in Confidential Search Mode are hard-excluded per §4.1.

## 16. Anti-gaming

| Board | Guard |
|---|---|
| Outreach | Distinct recipients only. Daily counted cap. Duplicate or near-duplicate bodies don't count. |
| Applications | Self-logged and trusted. Cap 15/day counted. Duplicate job IDs don't count twice. |
| Visibility | Minimum post length. One counted post per day. |
| Contribution | Must clear moderation **and** receive a reply or reaction from another member. |
| Action Score | Inherits every guard above. |

**Any action reversed, removed, or moderated retroactively decrements points.** Otherwise the fastest path to the top is spam plus deletion. This forces an append-only activity ledger rather than counters.

## 17. Badges

Top three on any weekly board earns a permanent, dated badge — *Top 3, Outreach, week of Aug 10.* Repeat placements show a count.

| Badge |
|---|
| Top 3 — Action · Outreach · Applications · Visibility · Contribution · Lifetime |

**These qualify for the Community gate** alongside Cleaned Up, Asked, Known, Backed, Consistent, Documented. A placement is unambiguously real work.

Visible on the leaderboard and Community profile. **Never on the Dossier** — that document goes to employers, and gamification has no place in it.

## 18. Wellbeing

**Never show a discouraging rank.** Top 10 publicly, plus own position only when inside the top 25. Below that, show progress toward the board: *"You're 40 points from the top 10 this week."* Never "you are 312th."

**Personal best runs alongside every board.** For most members, beating their own previous week is the more useful and less harmful comparison.

> **Your best week yet — 185 points.** Previous best: 160.

**The weekly reset is the point.** Everyone starts Monday at zero. Only the lifetime board accumulates.

**Never rank on outcomes.** No board for interviews, offers, or roles landed. Those depend on luck, market, and timing — and a board someone tops by getting hired is a board everyone else loses by not getting hired.

**Never send a losing notification.** Notify on badges earned and personal bests. Never "you dropped out of the top 10."

**Opt-out is one tap, always visible**, and removes them immediately and retroactively.

## 19. Placement

**My Stats** — all six boards, band filter, own rank, personal bests, badge case.
**Community page** — condensed module: top 3 of the current week's Action Score, link through to My Stats.
**Weekly Search Sprint** — one line at week's end: *"You finished at 140 points. Your best is 185."* No ranking inside the Sprint; the Sprint is about their plan, not the crowd.

## 20. Stats page additions

- **Lifetime Action Points** — the headline number
- Weekly points history trend
- Streak — consecutive Sprints completed
- Badge case, dated
- Lifetime activity totals — outreach, applications, posts, references
- Personal bests — best week, longest streak, most outreach in a week
- **Version conversion** per Resume Studio §3.9 — which resume version is actually converting

# PART FIVE — BUILD NOTES

- Store Confidential Search Mode as a member-level flag checked at **every render and send path**, not per feature. One missed check is a real-world harm to someone's livelihood.
- Test: no member in Confidential Search Mode appears in any leaderboard query, community name display, or public badge surface.
- Test: no outbound email to a member in this mode contains search-identifying language in the subject.
- Corporate-domain detection on both registration email and Gmail connection.
- Log mode changes with timestamps. If something leaks, we need the state at the time.
- Persona change from Worried to Laid off **prompts**, never auto-disables the mode.
- Leaderboards compute from an append-only activity ledger. Weekly boundary Monday 00:00 member-local. Recompute at most every 15 minutes — real-time ranking invites refresh-chasing. Ties break by earliest achievement.
