Goal: raise the number of direct (employer-own career system) jobs we can offer to Google, from about 189,000 to as close to all 355,000 as the employers' own sites allow. Dry run first; do NOT import into production until the owner approves the counts.

Why
- A job page is offered to Google only when it is on the employer's own career system AND the crawler last confirmed it open within 14 days AND its stored description is 1,500 bytes or more (`PUBLIC_JOB_VERIFIED_DAYS`, `PUBLIC_JOB_MIN_INDEX_DESCRIPTION`, `src/lib/jobs/public-job-page.ts`). Do not change those rules in this task.
- Measured 2026-11-13 from a 10% block sample of live jobs (full numbers in `docs/jobs-report/direct-index-gap-2026-11-13.md`, query `scripts/jobsreport/direct_index_gap.sql`). Of about 355,000 live direct jobs, about 189,000 (53%) are offered. The rest, as mutually exclusive buckets:
  - about 86,000 (24%) have good text but were last confirmed open 14 to 30 days ago. A server cron (`direct-reverify`) now re-checks these through the employer system's public API, but the crawler's own cadence is the real fix.
  - about 30,000 (8.6%) have no structured location at all (no `locationCountry`, no `locationState`), so the sitemap rule "in the US" drops them. Their location text is empty, "Remote" or "Multiple Locations". The crawler already screens out non-US jobs, so it should store country US (and the state when it has one) on what it keeps.
  - about 24,000 (6.9%) have NO description stored (null), and about 17,000 (4.9%) have a description under 1,500 bytes. `docs/specs/ncrawl-full-description-prompt.md` covers the stubs at RTX, BAE, Oracle, HPE, Marsh, Trane, PNC, Cisco and others; this task adds the null-description rows, a different failure.
  - about 4,200 reopened or reposted twice or more (a ghost-job rule; leave it), about 3,200 outside the US.
  - Of the 41,000 with missing or short text, most were confirmed open in the last 14 days, so they become offerable the moment the text is stored.
- The server does not drop text: `src/app/api/admin/jobs/import/route.ts` stores what the sender posts, and a payload with no description never erases one already held. The fixes are in the crawler.
- Method rule (CLAUDE.md "Employer discovery rule"): read the career site and the job system behind it, never the corporate site. Keep the polite rate and the identified user agent.

Do this, in order
0. Location (about 30,000 jobs). For direct rows with no `locationCountry` and no `locationState`, find which sources and hosts they come from, and why the location is not stored (list page has none, detail page does, the field is not mapped). Fix the adapters to store the country and the state, and report per host how many rows gain a US location. Do not set country US on a row you cannot show is in the US.
1. Missing text (about 45,000 fresh jobs). Employers with the most direct jobs confirmed this fortnight but no or short text, estimated: JPMorgan Chase 2,040, RTX 1,300, Apple 1,220, BAE Systems 940, Oracle 830, Hewlett Packard Enterprise 360, Google 260. For each: find where the adapter gets the description (list summary, header block, detail page, JSON-LD `JobPosting`), why it is null or short, and fix it to send the full plain-text body. JPMorgan is the largest single gap: say whether its rows are null or stubs and why. Then find which other hosts have null descriptions by counting null-description direct rows per host across all sources, and list the top 20.
2. Re-verification (about 82,000 jobs). Find why direct rows go 14+ days without a re-check. Report per source the re-crawl interval and the share of its rows confirmed within 14 days. The employers with the most stale direct rows, estimated: Raytheon Company 1,460, Deloitte 1,130, CACI 940, Microsoft 950, Sargent & Lundy 830, Publicis 760, Blue Origin 750, Northrop Grumman 720, Morgan Stanley 720, Booz Allen 650, Thermo Fisher 590, General Motors 570, PwC 540, Capital One 550, Leidos 520, Salesforce 480. Propose a cadence that keeps every direct row inside 14 days, and its request cost. A row the employer no longer lists should close through the normal liveness path, not be kept alive.
3. Staffing and recruiter boards. Owner decision 2026-11-13: staffing firms and recruiters are fine as long as the jobs are real.
