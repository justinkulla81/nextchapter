San Diego, CA: white-collar jobs and employment

Prepared for Point Loma Nazarene University by NextChapter Research · career-site data as of 2026-10-22 · draft

Summary

The sample is 1,036 white-collar postings from 47 employers' own career sites. 17% are health care, 1% are entry-level, 45% are senior individual contributor or above, and median stated pay is $154,900 a year.

  • Labor market: unemployment was 4.9% in 2026-08, against 5.1% a year earlier; nonfarm employment changed 0.7% over twelve months.
  • Entry-level: 15 white-collar postings across 7 employers.
  • AI: 33.4% of white-collar postings mention AI; those pay a median of $142,450 against $155,250 for the rest.
  • Concentration: Sharp HealthCare alone is 16% of all postings, so the mix reflects the largest readable employers more than the whole market.

Coverage

1,247 postings in the area were read from 47 employers, and 1,036 of them are white-collar (83%). Another 131 could not be classified from the title and are left out. The frame is every white-collar job at every level, entry-level included (Census major groups 1 and 2).

Employers on career systems we cannot read are missing, so read these figures as describing the employers listed, not every opening in the area.

Large employers we looked for and could not read: UC San Diego Health, University of California San Diego, San Diego State University, University of San Diego, San Diego Community College District, Rady Children's Health, Kaiser Permanente, Qualcomm, General Atomics, Intuit, Jack in the Box, Sempra, San Diego Gas & Electric, Biogen.

Method and limits

  • Source: employers' own career sites, crawled 2026-10-22, tagged by job title with fixed rules; titles that cannot be classified are excluded.
  • AI, remote, hybrid and soft-skill figures are keyword matches in posting text; pay medians use only postings that state pay.
  • Postings are not BLS job openings: one opening can have several postings and some are always open.
  • There is no history yet. Functions gaining or losing, reposts and ghost-job risk need at least two crawls.
  • Labor market data: BLS Local Area Unemployment Statistics and Current Employment Statistics bulk files, not seasonally adjusted.

Sources: employers' own career sites; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (LAUS, CES); O*NET. Figures describe the employers listed, not every opening in the area.
