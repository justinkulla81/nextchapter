# Draft job postings and hiring values

**Status: DRAFT, not for posting.** Nothing here has had legal review. Every `[bracket]` must be filled or removed before anything goes live. See "Before you post anything" at the end.

How to read the titles: **Lead** means you own a function and set direction. **Contributor** means you do the work inside a function someone else leads. Where a role has no lead yet, the contributor works directly with the founder.

---

## About NextChapter (shared blurb)

NextChapter helps professionals navigate a job search after a layoff or a career change. We combine a candidate platform (market reality report, resume tools, confidential search, coaching) with programs for employers, coaches, recruiters, colleges and workforce boards. We publish a monthly white-collar employment report. We are a small team in New York City. [Confirm wording and work location: office, hybrid or remote.]

## What we value (shared across every posting)

- **Emotional intelligence.** You read people well, listen first, and stay steady with someone who is stressed about their livelihood.
- **Demonstrated knowledge.** We care what you have actually done and learned, not where you studied or worked. Show us the work.
- **A point of view.** You form an opinion, say it plainly, and change it when the evidence changes.
- **Conscientiousness.** You finish what you start, follow up, and keep your commitments.
- **Integrity.** You tell the truth, including about numbers, claims and mistakes.
- **Ownership.** You take responsibility for outcomes and find a way to get things done.
- **Optimism.** You bring constructive energy to hard problems.
- **Creativity.** You find a new angle when the standard one is not working.
- **Care about the problem.** You are drawn to helping people through a hard career moment.
- **Team player with a view.** You collaborate well and still disagree openly.
- **Bonus: working knowledge of AI tools.** We use AI agents for first drafts and research, and people for judgment and relationships.

## Hiring rubric (use for every role)

Score each value 1–4 using the same questions and notes for every candidate. Decide as a team after scoring, not before.

| Value | Ask | Strong answer | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional intelligence | "Tell me about a time someone was upset with you at work. What did you do?" | Listens first, names the other person's view, resolves it | Blames, or avoids conflict |
| Demonstrated knowledge | "Walk me through a project you led and what you'd do differently." (Plus a short work sample.) | Specific, accurate, measurable | Vague or inflated |
| Point of view | "What is something people in your field get wrong?" | A clear opinion with evidence and openness to challenge | No view, or no openness |
| Conscientiousness | "Tell me about something you committed to that got hard." | Follows through; communicates early | Drops items; vague about follow-ups |
| Integrity | "Tell me about a time you found a mistake in your own work." | Reports it quickly and fixes it | Hides or minimizes |
| Ownership | "What is the hardest thing you got done without a lot of support?" | Specific actions and results | Waits for direction |
| Optimism | "Tell me about a setback and what you did next." | Constructive, specific next steps | Cynical, passive |
| Creativity | "Tell me about a problem you solved in an unusual way." | Original and practical | Generic |
| Care about the problem | "Why this problem?" | Personal, specific, informed | Generic or opportunistic |
| Team player with a view | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate." | Disagreed openly and respectfully; committed afterward | Always agrees, or won't commit |
| AI (bonus) | "How do you use AI tools in your work today?" | Specific uses, and where it fails | Hype or avoidance |

**Notes on wording and fairness**
- Avoid phrases such as "work hard, play hard," "unending hours," "digital native," "young and energetic," or "culture fit." They can discourage or screen out protected groups. We say "owns outcomes and gets it done" and score it with the questions above.
- Use a work sample instead of a pedigree filter. Pay people for substantial sample work, or keep it under a couple of hours.
- Do not use AI to score or rank applicants for NYC jobs without counsel (see below).
- Ask every candidate the same questions in the same order. Write notes before the debrief.

## Before you post anything

1. **Pay.** You will have an employee count that includes you and any contractors. NYC's pay-range rule applies at four or more. If it applies, post a good-faith range for each role. Check New York State's version too. Even if exempt, state the structure honestly.
2. **Full-time means employment.** That means payroll, workers' compensation, and wage and hour rules. Commission-only or equity-only full-time roles are a legal risk. Counsel should review before any role is offered.
3. **Equity.** Needs a lawyer for the grant, vesting and cliff.
4. **Coaches and recruiters.** Keep the language clear that this is not a job. Counsel should confirm the independent arrangement and written agreement.
5. **No AI screening of applicants** for NYC roles unless counsel confirms compliance with NYC Local Law 144 (bias audit, public summary, notice to candidates).
6. **Equal opportunity and accommodation statement.** Add a standard one. [Counsel to supply.]
7. **Competitors' ex-employees.** Ask about non-competes and client restrictions before discussing specifics.
