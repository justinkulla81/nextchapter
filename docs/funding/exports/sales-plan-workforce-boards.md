# How we make the first sales to workforce boards, state agencies and their partners

Written 2026-10-23 from the crawl of 1,714 buyer sites (531 workforce boards, 403 EDA districts, 729 local economic development groups, 51 state agencies), the layoff notices in each board's counties, and the competitor and funding research. Spreadsheet: `buyers-2026-10-23.xlsx` (tabs: Call first, All boards, Open RFPs, Vendors seen, Economic development & state, Job centers).

## What we found, in numbers

- **What boards actually buy.** 100 recent solicitations from 47 buyers. The recurring ones: One-Stop Operator contracts (19), career-services and case-management providers (13), WIOA adult and dislocated-worker providers (36), then youth, training and misc. Career-services and dislocated-worker contracts are the closest to what we sell. One-Stop Operator and youth contracts are not ours, but the winners are buyers or partners.
- **Who holds the technology seat.** Geographic Solutions' portal shows up on 65 buyer sites (58 of the 560 boards). FutureFit shows on none, Lightcast on 5, WIN Learning on none. Salesforce is on 43 (case-management builds). Nobody sells to boards the thing we sell: a professional-level layoff and career-pivot layer.
- **Layoffs are the opening.** Last 12 months, in the states where we have notices: Texas 40,738 workers (253 notices), California 21,034, Ohio 8,721, Michigan 8,136, North Carolina 6,385, Iowa 3,862. Boards with the most: Dallas 9,715, Panhandle (TX) 7,123, Gulf Coast 6,666, Tarrant 5,099, Greater Ohio 4,349, SEMCA (MI) 3,756, Los Angeles 3,734. Our notice data covers only some states, so this undercounts.
- **New money, now.** DOL announced 20 Reskill dislocated-worker awards on 2026-10-19: $95.5M across 17 states (AK, CA, CT, FL, KY, LA, ME, MI, NC, NE, NH, NY, OH, OK, RI, TX, WA). Those states must now buy AI-reskilling services. Overlap with the biggest layoff states: TX, CA, OH, MI, NC. Kentucky is ours to lose: $2.55M statewide, feeding the South Central board's AI Workforce Readiness Network RFP (proposals closed 2026-09-18, award notice expected now; the board's Q&A says an out-of-state for-profit may be prime).
- **Small purchases skip the RFP.** Boards that publish their rules set thresholds from $10,000 (micro-purchase) up to $150,000 to $250,000 (informal quotes or a small-purchase method). Dallas: up to $10,000 micro-purchase, up to $250,000 small purchase. Centralina (NC): $15,000, up to $50,000. Howard County (MD): informal quotes from $10,000 to $29,999. Only 31 boards post their rules, so ask each one.

## The five ways money reaches us, fastest first

1. **Small purchase (days to weeks).** A layoff-response pilot priced under the board's threshold: a public sign-up page and layoff seats for one announced layoff, plus a workshop series. No RFP. This is the first sale at every board.
2. **Subaward under a funded prime (weeks).** DOL Reskill states and the boards named in those awards need an AI-training and career-pivot delivery partner. Approach the state workforce agency's grant manager and the named local boards at the same time. Kentucky first.
3. **ETPL listing (months, then recurring).** One approved training program on a state's Eligible Training Provider List lets every board in that state pay for our courses with individual training accounts. Per-seat revenue, no sale per board. Apply first in states that are both high-layoff and Reskill states: Texas, California, Ohio, Michigan, North Carolina.
4. **Respond to an RFP (months).** Only when it is career services, career readiness, rapid-response workshops or an AI-reskilling network. Not One-Stop Operator or youth. Track the 87 rows on the Open RFPs tab and the recurring issuers.
5. **Through economic development partners (referrals).** EDA districts and local economic development groups hear about a layoff first. They do not buy; they introduce us and co-apply as prime on grants where we sub. Give them a layoff-response page they can hand to an employer.

## Opening a conversation (what the spreadsheet fills in for each board)

"Your counties had [N] layoff notices in the last year, [W] workers, including [employers]. Your site lists [RFP title]. We run a layoff-support layer for managers and professionals that sits beside [Geographic Solutions / your current system]: public sign-up page, coaching, career-pivot paths and AI reskilling, with consented outcome counts you can send to your state. Can we set one up for your next announced layoff as a pilot?"

Say plainly what we are not: not case management, not eligibility, not federal reporting. The competitor research found that boards do not want a second case-management system, and every board using Geographic Solutions will ask.

## What has to exist before a board can say yes

- **A price.** There is no public price. Placeholders in our notes are $15,000 to $40,000 per course build and $150 to $400 per participant, never tested. Decide a pilot price below $10,000 and a per-seat price, then test on two boards.
- **Proof.** One live pilot with outcome counts (One Louisville and KentuckianaWorks demos exist). References come from the first two buyers.
- **Paperwork.** Security questionnaire answers (the CASA work is the base), a one-page procurement packet (W-9, insurance, SOC 2 status, data handling), sole-source justification language boards can paste.
- **For-profit eligibility, checked per solicitation.** WIOA allows for-profit providers, but each RFP states its own rule, and about half of the rows on the Open RFPs tab say UNKNOWN until read.

## What this does not tell you yet

- We have not read the RFPs themselves, only found and listed them; scores are from titles.
- Layoff counts come from the states where we sync WARN notices.
- Vendor footprints are visible only where a buyer links to the vendor's portal. A board can use Geographic Solutions without linking it.
- Contract values and end dates for the incumbents are largely unknown; state contract databases and public-records requests are the way to get them.
