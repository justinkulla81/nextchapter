## Detail

### Lumina Foundation: VERIFIED (2026-10-13)
- Program: proactive grantmaking on postsecondary attainment; occasional open RFPs.
- Funds: postsecondary access and attainment, mostly with chosen partners.
- Who can apply: its grants page says grantmaking is primarily proactive and it "does not accept or respond to unsolicited requests/letters of inquiry". It occasionally issues open RFPs on specific topics. The pages read do not state eligible organization types. Older third-party listings say 501(c)(3) public charities (secondary, unconfirmed).
- For NextChapter: no direct route today. Watch for an RFP or be named by a partner it funds.
- Source: https://www.luminafoundation.org/grants/

### Strada Education Foundation: VERIFIED (2026-10-13)
- Programs: Beyond Completion Challenge, Intermediary Learning Collaborative grants, Innovation in Career Services grants (Employer and Community College Partnership Challenge ran 2022 to 2025).
- Funds: education-to-employment pathways, career services, employer and college partnerships.
- Who can apply: "We provide grants to nonprofit organizations, postsecondary education providers, and workforce and skills-based educators." Whether a for-profit counts as a skills-based educator, and whether applications are open or invited, is not stated on the page read.
- For NextChapter: possible, unconfirmed. Ask through a college partner first.
- Source: https://www.strada.org/how-we-work/grantmaking

### Walmart.org: VERIFIED (2026-10-13). EXCLUDES FOR-PROFITS
- Programs: Creating Opportunity priority area (invitation-only); Spark Good Local Community Grants.
- Who can apply: U.S. funding is generally limited to IRS-recognized public charities and equivalents. Most focus areas are invitation only: "Unsolicited proposals will not be accepted, and invitations are not granted upon request." Local Community Grants is the only program that takes applications all year.
- For NextChapter: not directly; only as a vendor inside a nonprofit or college grantee.
- Source: https://walmart.org/how-we-give/grant-eligibility

### Siegel Family Endowment: VERIFIED (2026-10-13). EXCLUDES FOR-PROFITS
- Program: Learning, Work and Infrastructure areas (future of work and AI).
- Who can apply: "Siegel only accepts proposals by invitation." Grants go to or through U.S. section 501(c)(3) public charities. Occasional open calls.
- For NextChapter: not directly; a 501(c)(3) partner would hold the grant.
- Source: https://www.siegelendowment.org/what-we-fund/

## What this means

- Of the four named starting funders, two (Lumina, Strada) do not state for-profit eligibility and two others found (Walmart.org, Siegel) exclude for-profits by their own wording. A for-profit gets these funds only inside a college or nonprofit grantee, which is the same pattern as the federal grants.
- The most practical first step is not to ask any of them for a grant. It is to list the colleges, boards and nonprofits NextChapter works with that already hold these funders' grants, and ask whether a NextChapter provider line fits their next proposal.
- Do not approach until the TO VERIFY rows are read on the funders' own pages.
